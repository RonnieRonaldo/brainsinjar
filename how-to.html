How to make a brain in a jar prop

The trick to this piece of DIY oddities and curiosities is getting the drawing close to the glass. Pick your jar first, then choose the print that fits its straight sides.

What you’ll need

Each of the three designs comes in three sizes, so most straight-sided kitchen jars are worth a try. Check the jam, peanut butter and mason jars in your cupboard before buying one.

  • A clean, dry, clear jar with its lid
  • The printable PDF, a color printer and US Letter or A4 paper
  • A ruler, scrap paper and scissors
  • Glue for the outer labels
  • Optional: lid paint or a permanent marker, plus a cotton swab and petroleum jelly for a curved shoulder

Measure first, then print

The smallest wrap measures about 11 × 4.3 in (28 × 11 cm). The largest suits taller mason jars up to about 7.5 in (19 cm) high.

Slip a strip of scrap paper inside the jar. Mark where the ends meet and measure the strip flat. Check the height from the base to where the glass starts curving inward. Select the closest wrap and print at actual size. Check its printed dimensions before cutting.

Fit the paper against the glass

Cut out the brain panel, roll it loosely with the printed side facing out and slide it through the opening. Let it spring open. Rotate the jar until the folds you like face forward. A small gap between the ends can face the wall.

If the shoulder slopes inward sharply, trim a narrow strip off the top so the wrap sits flat against the straight glass. To soften the clear band above the paper, rub a trace of petroleum jelly along the inside glass with a cotton swab. A tiny dab of butter also works for a temporary display. Keep it inside, above the paper, and leave the outside clean.

Give the lid a matching finish

If the lid already looks right, keep its original color. For this set I took three different lids off their jars and spray-painted the outsides black. You can also brush on a little paint or cover small lettering with a permanent marker. Let the finish dry before putting the lid back.

Place the specimen labels

Try the black label against the front while the jar is on its shelf. Leave enough of the brain visible above it to see the folds. Glue the label to the outside glass. Center the smaller Old Salem label on the lid, or put it on a second bottle if you like the lid plain.

Arrange your printable oddities

Set the finished brain beside the eyeball and mealworm jars, or give it a shelf of its own. Turn the seam to the back and look at the jar from the angle you’ll usually see it.
