Which Size Will Fit Your Jar?

The smallest wrap measures about 11 × 4.3 in (28 × 11 cm). The largest suits taller mason jars up to about 7.5 in (19 cm) high.

Jam canning jars and peanut butter jars are good places to start. With mason canning jars, measure the straight part inside and try a paper strip around it. Even a huge mason jar can work for a front-facing display if the illustration covers the side you see. A gap at the back is fine when the jar stays in one spot.

A Place for Your Brain Jar in a Cabinet of Curiosities

On a narrow gothic wall shelf, I’d give the brain a spot of its own. Turn it until a deep fold sits just above the label. In a larger Wunderkammer cabinet of curiosities, set the taller jar behind the eyeballs and mealworms so you can read all three labels from the front.

A small fake animal skull makes a good neighbor if you already have a taxidermy display. Slip a piece of gothic ephemera behind the jars, or use the spare witchy bottle labels to give an old bottle the same Old Salem lettering.

A Miniature Spider Terrarium for the Next Shelf

Spoon a little sand into a spare jar. Stand up two dry twigs with a few leaves still attached and stretch wisps of off-white thread between them for cobwebs. For another spooky specimen, fill a little bottle with imitation bones or thoroughly cleaned and dried bones.

Making a Brain in a Jar Prop for Halloween?

Try the smallest wrap in a jam jar on the party table. For a craft night, lay out all three designs and let everyone pick a specimen. When the evening is over, take your favorite one back to the oddities shelf.

Does the brain jar need liquid?

Keep the jar dry. The color and shadows are printed on the wrap. Once the paper curves against the glass, the folds look as though they fill the jar.

Can I use the printable specimen jar labels separately?

Yes. Cut the black Cerebrum Humanum label for the front of your brain jar. The white Old Salem label also fits on a lid or a spare apothecary bottle.
