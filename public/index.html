Inside the printable set

Start with the brain. The same PDF includes two extra specimens, a jar of eyeballs and a jar of mealworms. Each design comes in small, medium and large, giving you nine design-and-size combinations. You print the wraps and matching printable specimen jar labels at home and supply your own jars.

The black Cerebrum Humanum label names the specimen. The white Old Salem label can go on the lid, so you can still read it when you look down at the shelf.

Which jar fits your shelf?

The smallest wrap measures about 11 × 4.3 in (28 × 11 cm). The largest suits taller mason jars up to about 7.5 in (19 cm) high.

Jam canning jars and peanut butter jars are good places to start. With mason canning jars, measure the straight part inside and try a paper strip around it. Even a huge mason jar can work for a front-facing display if the illustration covers the side you see. A gap at the back is fine when the jar stays in one spot.

Two more specimens for the cabinet

Set the eyeballs beside the brain for a matching pair. The golden mealworms bring a different color to an oddities shelf. All three use the same Old Salem lettering, so even mismatched glassware looks like one collection.

Build a little curiosity cabinet

On a gothic wall shelf, stand the brain beside a small fake animal skull or some gothic ephemera. If you have a taxidermy display or a Wunderkammer cabinet of curiosities, try leaving the most detailed folds facing the room.

The witchy bottle labels also work on spare glassware. Add sand, a dry twig with a few leaves, and a strand of pale thread stretched between the branches for a miniature spider terrarium. Or fill a second jar with imitation bones or thoroughly cleaned, dried bones for another spooky specimen.
