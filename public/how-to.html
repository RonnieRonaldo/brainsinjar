How to Make a Brain in a Jar Prop

For this DIY brain in a jar, start with an empty jar from your cupboard. Hold it at shelf height and look through the front. The straight stretch of glass below the shoulder is where the printed folds will show best.

What you’ll need

Each of the three designs comes in three sizes, so most straight-sided kitchen jars are worth a try. Try a jam jar, a peanut butter jar or a clear mason jar. If you can see your fingers clearly through the side, you’ll be able to see the fine lines in the brain print there too.

  • A clean, dry, clear jar with its lid
  • The printable PDF, a color printer and US Letter or A4 paper
  • A ruler, scrap paper and scissors
  • Glue for the outer labels
  • Optional: lid paint or a permanent marker, plus a cotton swab and petroleum jelly for a curved shoulder

Measure first, then print

The smallest wrap measures about 11 × 4.3 in (28 × 11 cm). The largest suits taller mason jars up to about 7.5 in (19 cm) high.

Slip a strip of scrap paper inside the jar. Mark where the ends meet and measure the strip flat. Check the height from the base to where the glass starts curving inward. Choose the wrap closest to those measurements and print at actual size. Check its printed dimensions before cutting.

Fit the paper against the glass

Cut out the brain panel, roll it loosely with the printed side facing out and slide it through the opening. Let it spring open. Rotate the jar until the folds you like face forward. A small gap between the ends can face the wall.

If the shoulder slopes inward sharply, trim a narrow strip off the top so the wrap sits flat against the straight glass. To soften the clear band above the paper, rub a trace of petroleum jelly along the inside glass with a cotton swab. A tiny dab of butter also works for a temporary display. Keep it inside, above the paper, and leave the outside clean.

Give the lid a matching finish

If the lid already looks right, keep its original color. For this set I took three different lids off their jars and spray-painted the outsides black. You can also brush on a little paint or cover small lettering with a permanent marker. Let the finish dry before putting the lid back.

Place the specimen labels

Try the black label against the front while the jar is on its shelf. Leave enough of the brain visible above it to see the folds. Glue the label to the outside glass. Center the smaller Old Salem label on the lid, or put it on a second bottle if you like the lid plain.

Questions About Making the Brain Jar

Which print setting keeps the wrap the right size?

Choose actual size or 100% in the print dialog. Measure the smallest design on paper before trimming it. It should be about 11 in wide by 4.3 in high (28 × 11 cm).

Why does the top of my paper wrap buckle?

The glass may curve inward near the lid. Take the wrap out and trim a little from its height until the top edge rests below the curve. Try it in the jar again before trimming more.

What if the ends of the wrap leave a gap?

Face the gap toward the wall for a jar that stays on a shelf. For a party table where people can see every side, try the next wrap size or a narrower jar.

Should I glue the brain wrap to the glass?

Let the curled paper open against the inside wall first. Keeping it loose lets you rotate the picture and swap designs later. Use glue on the outside labels once you like their position.

Arrange Your Printable Oddities

Set the finished brain beside the eyeball and mealworm jars, or give it a shelf of its own. Try the tallest pot at the back and stand the little jam jar in front. A quarter turn can bring a whole fold into view above the label.
